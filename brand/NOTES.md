## What the design used as a placeholder
A gold 8-pointed star polygon, 64×64, stroke `#C8A046`, stroke-width 2.5.
This is a Damascene marquetry / tilework motif — it is already on-brand and
deliberately *urban* rather than desert.

## Iconographic grounding (see ../research/arsu.md)
Arsu is attested as a camel-mounted protector of caravans, in Roman-style
cuirass with spear and small round shield, sometimes with a crescent in the
field. His twin Azizos rides a horse. Rostovtzeff (1932) read the pair as the
evening and morning star — "the two stars who lead caravans in the desert".
That star reading is a widely repeated *interpretation*, not a certainty.

So both directions below are historically defensible:
- camel-and-rider = the literal attested relief
- twin stars = the Arsu/Azizos star pairing, and matches the design's own motif

## Candidates
Generated with `fal-ai/recraft/v4.1/text-to-vector` (true editable SVG output),
then recoloured to the design palette. Sources in `candidates/`, recoloured in
`recolored/`.

- `c7-coin-rider` — camel + rider in a coin/seal roundel with inner ring
- `c8-token-rider` — camel + rider with upright spear, rounded-square token
- `s1-twin-stars`  — two 8-pointed stars, unequal, overlapping (Arsu + Azizos)
- `s2-star-road`   — single 8-pointed star crossed by a gold road band

## Open concern
Camel/desert imagery is the cliché a Syrian audience most often rejects — Syria
reads urban and green (Damascus, Aleppo, the coast), and the design's own deep-green
palette leans that way deliberately. Flagged to the client rather than decided.
